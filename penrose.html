← Circle Puzzle Cuts: two disks, n up to 200

A Penrose tiling that turns itself into a fractal

Discs sit on the five-fold vertices of a Penrose tiling and turn in 72° steps. Every cut that any turn could ever carry somewhere new is added, round after round. Below the golden ratio the cuts close up into a finite puzzle; above it they never stop multiplying.

Edge length is 1, and neighbouring five-fold vertices sit φ² ≈ 2.618 apart. The switch from finite to endless sits at radius φ ≈ 1.618 in my runs, to within about 0.003. Press Animate to turn as many discs at once as possible: each step picks a random set of discs that don’t overlap one another and turns them all together, each 72° one way or the other. Turn a disc or tap one to move just that disc. Coloured pieces are the ones inside some disc; the plain rhombi outside every disc never move. Below φ the pieces stay crisp however long it runs. Above φ the cut set is only computed to a finite depth, so very long runs gradually mix colours across cuts that weren’t computed yet.

Each round applies all four nontrivial turns of every disc to the cuts added in the previous round. Cuts are kept as exact line segments and circular arcs; colour shows the round in which a cut first appeared. The computation stops at 22 rounds or 70,000 cuts.
